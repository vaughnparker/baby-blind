# 01 - Introduction to Blindfolded Solving

## Welcome

This repository is a structured training program for learning to solve a Rubik's cube
blindfolded, starting from absolute zero. If you work through it patiently and
systematically, you will be able to solve a Rubik's cube without looking at it.

This is not a quick process. Take your time. Enjoy it.

---

## Prerequisites

You should know how to solve a Rubik's cube with your eyes open before starting this
program. If you don't yet, here are some great resources:

* How to Solve a Rubik's Cube - WIRED: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-R0KrXvWbc
* How to Solve a Rubik's Cube - JPerm: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Ron6MN45LY
* Charlie Eggins's Tutorial series
    * Beginner Rubik's Cube Blindfolded Tutorial Part 1 | Lettering Scheme and Tracing: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fqIU6Plcjr0
    * Beginner Rubik's Cube Blindfolded Tutorial Part 2 | Cycle Breaking, Pairing, and Wording: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVefINLblEw
    * Beginner Rubik's Cube Blindfolded Tutorial Part 3 | Execution: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bYQ3cD5s5no
    * Beginner Rubik's Cube Blindfolded Tutorial Part 4 | Example Solve: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wqa2wwSN3AE

---

## What is Blindfolded Solving?

In a blindfolded solve, the solver is allowed to inspect the cube with their eyes open
for a period of time (called **inspection** or **memo**), then puts on a blindfold and
solves the cube entirely from memory.

The key insight is that you don't need to be able to *see* the cube to solve it — you
just need to have *memorized* where every piece needs to go. The solve itself becomes
a purely mechanical process of executing algorithms you have practiced until they are
muscle memory.

---

## What Method Does This Repo Use?

This repo teaches **Old Pochmann (OP)**, the most beginner-friendly blindfolded solving
method. Old Pochmann works by solving one piece at a time, using just a small number of
algorithms that you likely already know from learning to solve the cube normally.

The best introduction to Old Pochmann is this video by JPerm:
- [JPerm - How to Solve a Rubik's Cube Blindfolded](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZZ41gWvltT8)

We strongly recommend watching this video before or alongside working through this repo.
This repo is loosely based on JPerm's tutorial, but adds a structured progression system
on top of it.

> **Note for advanced learners:** The scrambles in Phase 1 of this repo are also useful
> for people learning more advanced blindfolded methods such as Orozco or 3-Style. The
> recognition and tracing skills trained in Phase 1 are method-agnostic.

---

## The Speffz Lettering Scheme

Old Pochmann requires you to assign a letter to each sticker on the cube, so that you
can describe the cube's state as a sequence of letters — your **memo**. This repo uses
the **Speffz** lettering scheme, which is the most widely used standard.

In Speffz, each of the 24 edge stickers and 24 corner stickers on the cube is assigned
a letter from A to X (Y and Z are not used). You will learn what each letter looks like
on the cube as you work through Phase 1 of this repo.

For a visual reference:
- [Speffz diagram image](https://www.speedsolving.com/wiki/images/3/36/Speffz-333-western.png)
- [Speffz on the Speedsolving Wiki](https://www.speedsolving.com/wiki/index.php/Speffz)

---

## The Three Algorithms You Need

Old Pochmann requires only three algorithms. You do not need to learn all three at once
— this repo will tell you when to introduce each one.

**T Perm** (used for edges):
```
R U R' U' R' F R2 U' R' U' R U R' F'
```

**Modified Y Perm** (used for corners):
```
R U' R' U' R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R
```

**Ra Perm** (used for parity):
```
R U' R' U' R U R D R' U' R D' R' U2 R' U'
```

> **When do you need each algorithm?**
> - Phase 1A: T Perm only
> - Phase 1B: Modified Y Perm only
> - Phase 2A and 2B: T Perm and Modified Y Perm
> - Phase 2C onwards: T Perm, Modified Y Perm, and Ra Perm

---

## How to Use This Repo

The learning path is structured into two phases:

**Phase 1 - Recognition Training**
- Phase 1A: Edge recognition
- Phase 1B: Corner recognition

**Phase 2 - Memo Retention Training**
- Phase 2A: Constructed short memos, no parity
- Phase 2B: Natural length memos, no parity
- Phase 2C: Parity introduced
- Phase 2D: Full unrestricted solves

### How to Progress Through Levels

Each level contains 500 scrambles. You do not need to do all 500. Instead, work through
scrambles until you feel confident — a good rule of thumb is **10 successful solves in
a row** before moving to the next level. That said, trust your own feel. If you feel
ready, move on. If you don't, keep practicing.

There is no timer here. Accuracy and confidence matter more than speed at this stage.

Baby (read: beginner) 3BLD solver

**If you are here to learn blindfolded solving, start here: [01 - Introduction](01_intro.md)**

---

## Original Vision

This project started as a program to teach users how to solve a 3x3 Rubik's cube
blindfolded. The end goal was a structured series of scrambles with progressively
increasing difficulty, starting from a very small subset of letters and slowly
expanding to the full set.

The original letter difficulty system for edges:

```
Edge Letters: A_CD EFGH IJKL _NOP QRST UVWX

Level 1: ACD
Level 2: ACD UVWX
Level 3: ACD UVWX LR JT
Level 4: ACD UVWX LR JT FH NP
Level 5: ACD UVWX LR JT FH NP EIQ
Level 6: ACD UVWX LR JT FH NP EIQ KS GO
```

### The Vision

1. The human picks a level
2. The computer generates a random string of letters from the letter set of the
   selected level
3. The computer simplifies that random string until it is something you would
   actually memorize
4. The computer finds the scrambled cube that would result from applying those
   letters backwards
5. The computer converts that scrambled cube into a friendlier format
6. The computer applies Kociemba's algorithm, or some other algorithm, to find
   the scramble sequence that results in that cube
7. The computer gives the human the scramble sequence
8. The human applies the scramble sequence, and then solves the cube and times
   themselves
9. This process repeats dozens or hundreds of times until the human feels
   confident with all of the letters in the level, and then the human manually
   chooses to advance to the next level

---

## What Has Been Built

The original vision has been fully realized and expanded:

- A complete cube simulation and Old Pochmann solver in Python
- A Kociemba-based scramble generator that produces scrambles with controlled
  memo difficulty
- **Phase 1** (Recognition Training): 6 levels of edge-only scrambles and 6 levels
  of corner-only scrambles, using progressively larger letter sets
- **Phase 2** (Memo Retention Training):
  - Phase 2A: Constructed short memos (2-12 letters, no parity)
  - Phase 2B: Natural length memos (16-22 letters, no parity)
  - Phase 2C: Parity guaranteed (2 and 16 letters)
  - Phase 2D: Full unrestricted solves
- A complete set of student-facing guides walking a complete beginner through the
  entire learning path

# Memo Length Distribution Analysis

## Methodology

We generated 10,000 random scrambles using `new_scrambled_cube()` and solved each one using
`solve_cube()`. The solution string format is `<edge_memo><separator><corner_memo>`, where the
separator is `.` for no parity and `|` for parity. We then measured the edge memo length, corner
memo length, total memo length, and whether parity was present.

## Results (10,000 samples)

### Total memo length
- Min: 14
- Max: 26
- Mean: 20.32

### Edge memo length
- Min: 6
- Max: 17
- Mean: 12.15

### Corner memo length
- Min: 4
- Max: 13
- Mean: 8.16

### Parity
- 5014/10000 solves had parity (50.1%)

### Distribution of total memo length
```
 14:  (4)
 15:  (0)
 16:  (192)
 17:  (0)
 18: ######### (1868)
 19:  (0)
 20: ####################### (4604)
 21:  (0)
 22: ############## (2843)
 23:  (0)
 24: ## (469)
 25:  (0)
 26:  (20)
```

## Key Observations

1. **Total memo length is always even.** No odd totals appear in the distribution at all. This
makes sense because Old Pochmann always solves pieces in pairs — each swap involves 2 pieces —
so the total memo length must always be even.

2. **The distribution is roughly bell-shaped**, peaking at 20 letters total, which represents
the "typical" full solve.

3. **Parity occurs in almost exactly 50% of solves**, which is consistent with theoretical
expectations.

4. **The vast majority of solves (over 90%) fall between 18 and 22 total letters.** The extremes
(14 and 26) are very rare.

## Implications for Phase 2 Level Design

Based on this data, a natural progression for Phase 2 (memo retention training) might be:

```
Level 1:  2-4  total letters  (very short, almost solved cube)
Level 2:  6-8  total letters
Level 3:  10-12 total letters
Level 4:  14-16 total letters
Level 5:  18-20 total letters (approaching typical full solve)
Level 6:  22-26 total letters (full difficulty)
```

Parity should be avoided in the early levels (1-3) since it adds execution complexity on top
of memo complexity. It can be gradually introduced in levels 4 and above, once the student
is comfortable holding a longer memo.

Note that 6 is an arbitrary number of levels here — the actual number should be tuned based
on student feedback and experience.

# 02 - Phase 1 Overview: Recognition Training

## What is Recognition?

Before you can solve the cube blindfolded, you need to be able to look at any sticker
on the cube and instantly know its Speffz letter. This skill is called **recognition**,
and it is the foundation of everything else in blindfolded solving.

It sounds simple, but it takes real practice. When you first start, you will need to
think carefully about each sticker. But with enough repetition, recognition becomes
automatic — you look at a sticker and the letter comes to you immediately, without
thinking.

This is the goal of Phase 1.

---

## What is Tracing?

Recognition alone is not enough. You also need to be able to **trace** a cycle of
pieces. Tracing means following a chain of pieces from the buffer, through each target,
and back to the buffer — building up your memo letter by letter as you go.

If you have watched JPerm's blindfolded tutorial, you have already seen this process.
Phase 1 is about drilling it until it is second nature.

---

## How Phase 1 is Structured

Phase 1 is split into two sub-phases:

**Phase 1A - Edges**

You will practice tracing edges only. The corners of the cube will always be solved
when you apply a Phase 1A scramble, so you only need to worry about the edges.

You will start with a very small set of edge letters and gradually expand that set
level by level, until you are comfortable with all 22 edge letters.

You only need the **T Perm** for Phase 1A.

**Phase 1B - Corners**

You will practice tracing corners only. The edges of the cube will always be solved
when you apply a Phase 1B scramble, so you only need to worry about the corners.

Just like Phase 1A, you will start with a small set of corner letters and gradually
expand level by level.

You only need the **Modified Y Perm** for Phase 1B.

---

## How to Use the Scramble Files

Each level contains a text file with 500 scrambles, one per line. Here is how to
practice with them:

1. **Start with a solved cube.**
2. **Apply the scramble** from the file to your cube.
3. **Inspect the cube** and build your memo. Write it down if you need to at first
   — that is completely fine. Over time, try to hold it in your head.
4. **Put on your blindfold** (or just close your eyes).
5. **Execute your memo** — apply the setup moves and algorithms for each letter.
6. **Remove your blindfold** and check if the cube is solved.

---

## What Counts as a Successful Solve?

A solve is successful if and only if **the cube is fully solved at the end**. That
means every sticker is in its correct position.

It does not matter how long it took. It does not matter if you had to think hard about
a setup move. It does not matter if you wrote down your memo. What matters is that the
cube is solved at the end.

As you progress, you will naturally get faster and more confident. But for now, focus
purely on accuracy.

---

## How to Progress Through Levels

Work through scrambles at your own pace. When you feel confident at a level, move on.

A good rule of thumb is **10 successful solves in a row** before advancing to the next
level. But trust your own feel — if you feel ready earlier, move on. If 10 in a row
doesn't feel like enough, keep going.

There is no rush. The goal is confidence, not speed.

---

## A Note on Advanced Methods

If you are planning to eventually learn a more advanced blindfolded method such as
**Orozco** or **3-Style**, the skills you build in Phase 1 are directly transferable.
Recognition and tracing are method-agnostic — the letter system, the piece locations,
and the tracing process are the same regardless of which algorithms you use to execute
the solve.

Phase 1 of this repo is a solid foundation for any blindfolded method.

---

# 05 - Bridge: From Phase 1 to Phase 2

## Congratulations

You have completed Phase 1. You can now recognize and trace every edge and corner
piece on the cube. This is a significant achievement — most people who attempt
blindfolded solving never get this far.

Take a moment to appreciate what you have built. You have gone from knowing nothing
about blindfolded solving to having a complete mental map of every piece on the cube.

---

## What You Have Accomplished

By completing Phase 1, you have developed two fundamental skills:

1. **Recognition** — you can look at any sticker on the cube and immediately know
   its Speffz letter.

2. **Tracing** — you can follow a cycle of pieces from the buffer, through each
   target, and back to the buffer, building up your memo letter by letter.

These skills are the foundation of everything that follows.

---

## Before Moving to Phase 2

You might be tempted to try a full scramble — one where both edges and corners need
to be solved — before moving on. That is understandable, but we recommend waiting.

A full solve introduces two new challenges that Phase 1 did not prepare you for:

1. **Longer memos** — holding both edge and corner letters in your head at the same
   time is significantly harder than holding one or the other.
2. **Parity** — a situation that occurs in roughly half of all solves, requiring a
   third algorithm (the Ra Perm) that you have not yet learned.

Phase 2 will introduce both of these challenges gradually and in a controlled way.
Trust the process and move on to Phase 2 when you feel ready.

---

## What Phase 2 is About

In Phase 1, the difficulty was controlled by restricting which pieces were scrambled.
In Phase 2, all pieces are in play — but the difficulty is now controlled by the
**length of your memo**.

You will start with very short memos and gradually work up to full-length solves. By
the end of Phase 2, you will be doing complete, unrestricted blindfolded solves.

## The Edge Buffer

In Old Pochmann edges, the buffer is the **UR edge** (the edge between the U face and
the R face). In Speffz, this edge has two stickers: **B** (the U face sticker) and
**M** (the R face sticker). Because this is the buffer, you will never memorize B or M
— they are excluded from all edge levels.

This leaves 22 edge letters to learn: A C D E F G H I J K L N O P Q R S T U V W X.

the previous level. By the end of Level 6, you will be comfortable with all 22 edge

# 06 - Phase 2 Overview: Memo Retention Training

## What is Memo Retention?

In Phase 1, you practiced recognizing and tracing pieces. The scrambles were designed
to keep your memo short and manageable — you were only ever tracing edges or corners,
never both at the same time.

In Phase 2, the training wheels come off. You will now be tracing both edges and
corners in the same solve. The challenge is no longer *which* pieces to recognize —
you already know all of them. The challenge is now *how many* you can hold in your
head at once.

This skill is called **memo retention**, and it is what separates a solver who can
do short solves from one who can do full, unrestricted blindfolded solves.

---

## How Phase 2 is Structured

Phase 2 is split into four sub-phases:

**Phase 2A - Constructed Short Memos (no parity)**
Scrambles are constructed so that your total memo is very short — starting at just
2 letters and building up to 12. This bridges the gap between Phase 1 and full-length
solves. You only need the T Perm and Modified Y Perm.

**Phase 2B - Natural Memos, No Parity (no parity)**
Scrambles are fully random, but filtered so that parity never occurs and memo length
falls within a specific range. You will work through four memo length brackets:
16, 18, 20, and 22 letters. You only need the T Perm and Modified Y Perm.

**Phase 2C - Parity Introduced (parity guaranteed)**
For the first time, you will encounter parity in every single scramble. The memo
lengths are kept short and familiar so that you can focus entirely on learning to
recognize and execute the Ra Perm. This is where you learn the third and final
algorithm.

**Phase 2D - Full Difficulty**
No restrictions. Fully random scrambles, any memo length, parity or not. This is
a real blindfolded solve. If you can complete 10 in a row successfully, you are a
blindfolded solver.

---

## What is Parity?

Parity is a situation that occurs in roughly half of all blindfolded solves. You will
know you have parity if, after tracing all your edges, you have memorized an **odd
number of edge letters**.

When parity occurs, you must execute the **Ra Perm** after solving your edges and
before solving your corners. If you do not, your corners will not solve correctly.

The Ra Perm is:
```
R U' R' U' R U R D R' U' R D' R' U2 R' U'
```

Do not worry about learning the Ra Perm yet — Phase 2A and 2B are designed so that
parity never occurs. You will not need the Ra Perm until Phase 2C.

---

## How to Progress Through Levels

The same criterion applies as in Phase 1: **10 successful solves in a row** before
moving to the next level. Trust your own feel — if you feel ready earlier, move on.
If you need more practice, keep going.

# 08 - Phase 2B: Natural Memos, No Parity

## What is Phase 2B?

In Phase 2A, scrambles were constructed to have a specific memo length. In Phase 2B,
scrambles are fully random — but filtered so that parity never occurs and your memo
length falls within a specific range.

This is an important transition. For the first time, you are solving scrambles that
were not specially constructed for you. The cube is in a genuinely random state. The
only concession is that parity has been filtered out, so you still only need the
**T Perm** and **Modified Y Perm**.

---

## What to Expect

Based on analysis of 10,000 random scrambles, the distribution of memo lengths for
a fully random blindfolded solve looks like this:

```
14 letters:  4 solves    (0.04%)
16 letters:  192 solves  (1.92%)
18 letters:  1868 solves (18.68%)
20 letters:  4604 solves (46.04%)
22 letters:  2843 solves (28.43%)
24 letters:  469 solves  (4.69%)
26 letters:  20 solves   (0.20%)
```

Note that memo length is always even. This is a consequence of the cube's permutation
structure — any valid scramble can only be solved with an even total number of swaps,
which means your memo will always have an even number of letters. This is actually a
useful error-checking tool: if you finish tracing and your edge count and corner count
are not both even or both odd, you have made a mistake in tracing and should retrace
before putting on your blindfold.

The vast majority of full solves (over 90%) fall between 18 and 22 letters. Level 4
of Phase 2B — 22 letters — is therefore close to a typical full solve in terms of
memo length.

naturally occurring memo lengths.

in a fully random solve.

### Level 4: 22 total letters

# 09 - Phase 2C: Parity Introduced

## What is Phase 2C?

In Phase 2C, every single scramble has parity. The memo lengths are kept short and
familiar — just 2 or 16 letters total — so that you can focus entirely on one new
challenge: learning to detect and execute parity.

This is where you learn the **Ra Perm**, the third and final algorithm you need for
Old Pochmann.

---

## What is Parity?

Parity is a situation that occurs in roughly half of all blindfolded solves. It is a
consequence of the cube's permutation structure — specifically, it arises when the
edge permutation and corner permutation are both odd.

In practical terms, you will know you have parity by counting your memo letters:

- If your edge memo has an **odd** number of letters, you have parity.
- If your edge memo has an **even** number of letters, you do not have parity.

Your corner memo count will always agree with your edge memo count — both will be odd
(parity) or both will be even (no parity). If one is odd and the other is even, you
have made a mistake in tracing and should retrace before putting on your blindfold.

---

## What to Do When You Have Parity

If you have parity, you must execute the **Ra Perm** after solving your edges and
before solving your corners:

```
R U' R' U' R U R D R' U' R D' R' U2 R' U'
```

The order is:
1. Solve edges (T Perm)
2. Execute Ra Perm
3. Solve corners (Modified Y Perm)

If you do not execute the Ra Perm when you have parity, your corners will not solve
correctly.

---

## A Note on Solve Order

This guide recommends solving edges first, then corners. This is the most common
approach and is what JPerm's tutorial teaches.

However, it is not required. You could solve corners first and edges second. If you
do, parity detection works the same way — just check your corner memo count instead
of your edge memo count. If your corner memo has an odd number of letters, you have
parity, and you must execute the Ra Perm after solving corners and before solving
edges.

Whichever order you choose, be consistent — pick one and stick with it.

---

## The Levels

### Level 1: 2 total letters (parity guaranteed)
Your memo will contain exactly 2 letters in total — 1 edge and 1 corner. This is the
simplest possible parity case. The memo burden is minimal so you can focus entirely
on recognizing parity and executing the Ra Perm correctly.

### Level 2: 16 total letters (parity guaranteed)
Your memo will contain exactly 16 letters in total. This is a more realistic memo
length, similar to Phase 2B Level 1. By this point you should be comfortable
detecting and executing parity.

---

## How to Use the Scramble Files

1. **Start with a solved cube.**
2. **Apply the scramble** from the file to your cube.
3. **Inspect the cube** and build your memo — edges first, then corners.
4. **Count your edge letters** — they will always be odd in this phase.
5. **Put on your blindfold.**
6. **Solve edges** using the T Perm.
7. **Execute the Ra Perm.**
8. **Solve corners** using the Modified Y Perm.
9. **Remove your blindfold** and check if the cube is solved.

Work through the levels in order, starting with
`memo_length_2_scrambles.txt`. Use the **10 successful solves in a row** criterion
before moving to the next level.

# 10 - Phase 2D: Full Difficulty

## What is Phase 2D?

Phase 2D is the final phase. There are no restrictions — scrambles are fully random,
memo lengths vary from 14 to 26 letters, and parity occurs in roughly half of all
solves.

This is a real blindfolded solve.

---

## What to Expect

You have all the tools you need:

- **T Perm** for edges
- **Modified Y Perm** for corners
- **Ra Perm** for parity

Every scramble in this phase is a complete test of everything you have built across
Phase 1 and Phase 2. Some solves will feel easy. Some will feel hard. That is normal
— it reflects the natural variation in scramble difficulty.

Refer to the distribution from Phase 2B if you want a reminder of what memo lengths
to expect. The most common memo length is 20 letters, and roughly half of all solves
will have parity.

---

## How to Use the Scramble Files

1. **Start with a solved cube.**
2. **Apply the scramble** from the file to your cube.
3. **Inspect the cube** and build your memo — edges first, then corners.
4. **Count your edge letters** — if odd, you have parity.
5. **Put on your blindfold.**
6. **Solve edges** using the T Perm.
7. **If parity: execute the Ra Perm.**
8. **Solve corners** using the Modified Y Perm.
9. **Remove your blindfold** and check if the cube is solved.

---

## A Closing Note

If you have worked through this repo from the beginning and can complete 10 Phase 2D
solves in a row successfully, you are a blindfolded solver.

That is not a small thing. Blindfolded solving is one of the most impressive skills
in the cubing world, and you built it systematically, one letter at a time.

From here, the sky is the limit. You might want to work on speed, or explore more
advanced methods like M2, Orozco, or 3-Style. But whatever you do next, you have a
solid foundation to build on.

Congratulations.
