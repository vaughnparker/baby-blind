# 02 - Phase 1 Overview: Recognition Training

## What is Recognition?

Before you can solve the cube blindfolded, you need to be able to look at any sticker
on the cube and instantly know its Speffz letter. This skill is called **recognition**,
and it is the foundation of everything else in blindfolded solving.

It sounds simple, but it takes real practice. When you first start, you will need to
think carefully about each sticker. But with enough repetition, recognition becomes
automatic — you look at a sticker and the letter comes to you immediately, without
thinking.

This is the goal of Phase 1.

---

## What is Tracing?

Recognition alone is not enough. You also need to be able to **trace** a cycle of
pieces. Tracing means following a chain of pieces from the buffer, through each target,
and back to the buffer — building up your memo letter by letter as you go.

If you have watched JPerm's blindfolded tutorial, you have already seen this process.
Phase 1 is about drilling it until it is second nature.

---

## How Phase 1 is Structured

Phase 1 is split into two sub-phases:

**Phase 1A - Edges**

You will practice tracing edges only. The corners of the cube will always be solved
when you apply a Phase 1A scramble, so you only need to worry about the edges.

You will start with a very small set of edge letters and gradually expand that set
level by level, until you are comfortable with all 22 edge letters.

You only need the **T Perm** for Phase 1A.

**Phase 1B - Corners**

You will practice tracing corners only. The edges of the cube will always be solved
when you apply a Phase 1B scramble, so you only need to worry about the corners.

Just like Phase 1A, you will start with a small set of corner letters and gradually
expand level by level.

You only need the **Modified Y Perm** for Phase 1B.

---

## How to Use the Scramble Files

Each level contains a text file with 500 scrambles, one per line. Here is how to
practice with them:

1. **Start with a solved cube.**
2. **Apply the scramble** from the file to your cube.
3. **Inspect the cube** and build your memo. Write it down if you need to at first
   — that is completely fine. Over time, try to hold it in your head.
4. **Put on your blindfold** (or just close your eyes).
5. **Execute your memo** — apply the setup moves and algorithms for each letter.
6. **Remove your blindfold** and check if the cube is solved.

---

## What Counts as a Successful Solve?

A solve is successful if and only if **the cube is fully solved at the end**. That
means every sticker is in its correct position.

It does not matter how long it took. It does not matter if you had to think hard about
a setup move. It does not matter if you wrote down your memo. What matters is that the
cube is solved at the end.

As you progress, you will naturally get faster and more confident. But for now, focus
purely on accuracy.

---

## How to Progress Through Levels

Work through scrambles at your own pace. When you feel confident at a level, move on.

A good rule of thumb is **10 successful solves in a row** before advancing to the next
level. But trust your own feel — if you feel ready earlier, move on. If 10 in a row
doesn't feel like enough, keep going.

There is no rush. The goal is confidence, not speed.

---

## A Note on Advanced Methods

If you are planning to eventually learn a more advanced blindfolded method such as
**Orozco** or **3-Style**, the skills you build in Phase 1 are directly transferable.
Recognition and tracing are method-agnostic — the letter system, the piece locations,
and the tracing process are the same regardless of which algorithms you use to execute
the solve.

Phase 1 of this repo is a solid foundation for any blindfolded method.
